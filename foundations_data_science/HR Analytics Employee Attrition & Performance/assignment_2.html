Employee Performance and Retention Analysis

¶

Objectives¶

The primary goal of this report is to comprehensively analyze and prepare datasets for use in predictive modeling to improve employee retention and performance. This report focuses on the Data Understanding Phase of the CRISP-DM framework, aiming to:

  1. Assess Data Relevance:

    • Confirm that the datasets align with the goals of predicting employee attrition and improving performance.
    • Ensure the datasets cover key attributes such as job satisfaction, performance ratings, and demographics.

  2. Evaluate Data Quality:

    • Identify issues such as missing values, duplicate records, and inconsistencies.
    • Assess the completeness, accuracy, and usability of the data.

  3. Describe Data Characteristics:

    • Provide an overview of the datasets, including size, structure, and key statistics.
    • Summarize relationships between variables relevant to the data mining goals.

  4. Prepare Data for Modeling:

    • Resolve data quality issues through cleaning, standardization, and imputation.
    • Explore relationships and distributions to identify trends and anomalies.

  5. Document Findings:

    • Ensure transparency by documenting data sources, quality issues, and resolutions.
    • Provide insights into the impact of data quality on the predictive modeling phase.

By addressing these objectives, this report ensures that the datasets are reliable, clean, and ready for the next phases of analysis and modeling.

1. Data Collection¶

Overview¶

The datasets used for this study were acquired from Kaggle's HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance repository. The files include information on employee demographics, job performance, satisfaction, education, and more. These datasets provide a comprehensive foundation for exploring the factors affecting employee retention and performance.

Datasets¶

The following datasets were downloaded for analysis:

  1. Employee.csv: Employee demographic and job-related details.
  2. PerformanceRating.csv: Historical performance evaluations.
  3. SatisfiedLevel.csv: Satisfaction levels mapped to descriptive categories.
  4. EducationLevel.csv: Educational qualifications.
  5. RatingLevel.csv: Performance rating categories.
  6. DimDate.txt: Calendar-related information for time-series analysis.

Key Insights¶

Based on the visualizations, the following insights have been identified:

  1. Demographics: Age Distribution

    • The workforce predominantly consists of employees aged 20–30, indicating a focus on younger to middle-aged talent. This could suggest targeted hiring practices or challenges in retaining older employees. HR strategies might need to include initiatives aimed at supporting mid-to-late career employees.

  2. Job Satisfaction and Compensation

    • Employees with higher job satisfaction levels tend to earn more competitive salaries, as evidenced by the boxplot. This suggests that financial incentives play a crucial role in job satisfaction, making it imperative to design salary structures that reward high performance and loyalty.

  3. Departmental Attrition

    • Attrition rates are notably higher in Sales and Marketing compared to other departments. This may reflect higher job stress or dissatisfaction in these areas. HR should focus on providing better support, such as stress management programs, role-specific training, and tailored career progression plans.

  4. Correlations Between Variables

    • The heatmap highlights several important relationships:
      • Positive Correlation: Stability in roles is indicated by the strong link between YearsWithCurrManager and YearsAtCompany.
      • Negative Correlation: A clear inverse relationship between Attrition and metrics like job satisfaction reinforces the importance of maintaining employee satisfaction to reduce turnover.
      • Low Impact Variables: Factors such as StockOptionLevel show minimal impact on attrition or satisfaction, suggesting they may have limited relevance in predictive modeling.

  5. Work-Life Balance

    • The majority of employees rate their work-life balance as "satisfactory" or above. However, those who rate it poorly highlight a subset of the workforce at risk for disengagement or attrition. Enhancing work-life balance initiatives could improve overall satisfaction.
